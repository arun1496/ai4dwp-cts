## The One Formula to Remember

Good prompts follow this pattern:

> **Act as [role] + Do [task] + Using [context] + In [format]**

You don't need all four parts every time, but the more you include, the better the result.

| Part | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Act as** | Tell Copilot who it's writing for | *"As a finance analyst..."* |
| **Do** | The actual task | *"...summarise this report..."* |
| **Using** | The source or context | *"...using the document I have open..."* |
| **In** | The format you want back | *"...in five bullet points."* |

Put together: **"Act as a finance analyst, summarise the key risks in this report using the document I have open, in five bullet points."**

Write that on a sticky note. It works across Outlook, Word, Excel, and Teams.

## Copilot in Outlook

Copilot in Outlook helps you deal with your inbox faster and write better emails with less effort. Find it by opening an email and looking for the Copilot button in the toolbar, or by clicking **New Email** and selecting Copilot.

### Example prompts to try

**1. Summarise a long email thread**
> *"Summarise this email thread. What has been agreed, what is still outstanding, and who is responsible for what?"*

Use this when you've come back from leave, been added mid-thread, or just need the key points without reading twenty replies.

---

**2. Draft a reply for you to edit**
> *"Draft a reply to this email. Acknowledge the request, say we need three working days to review, and keep the tone professional but friendly."*

Copilot writes a first draft. You read it, adjust the tone or any details, then send. Never send without reading.

---

**3. Prepare for a meeting from your emails**
> *"Look at my emails from the last week related to the Q3 budget review. What are the main topics being discussed and are there any unresolved questions I should raise in the meeting?"*

You don't need to open any specific email first — just type this into the Copilot chat and it will go through your inbox on your behalf. Useful before a recurring meeting so you walk in already up to speed.

## Copilot in Word

Copilot in Word helps you draft, rewrite, and summarise documents. Open it from the **Home** ribbon (look for the Copilot icon), or right-click selected text.

### Example prompts to try

**1. Get a summary of a long document**
> *"Summarise this document in plain English. Give me the five most important points and flag anything that needs a decision."*

Works well on policy documents, reports, or anything someone has sent you to review before a meeting.

---

**2. Rewrite a section to be clearer**
> *"Rewrite the selected paragraph so it's easier to understand for someone who isn't a finance specialist. Keep it under 100 words."*

Select the paragraph first, then type this. Useful when you need to send something to a non-specialist colleague or a client.

---

**3. Draft a first version of a document**
> *"Draft a one-page project update for the Finance leadership team. The project is the Q3 cost-reduction review. Key points to include: the review is on track, three departments have responded, we are waiting on two more, and the final report will be ready by 22 August."*

Copilot writes the structure and wording. You fill in any gaps and check the facts. Think of it as a starting point, not a finished product.

## Copilot in Excel

Copilot in Excel helps you make sense of data without needing to know complex formulas. Open it from the **Home** ribbon when you have a spreadsheet open.

**One thing to check first:** Copilot works best when your data is set up as an Excel table. If you open it and nothing happens, click anywhere inside your data, press **Ctrl+T**, then press **Enter** when the box appears. That's it — you only need to do this once per spreadsheet, and Excel will remember. If you're not sure whether your spreadsheet is already set up this way, try Ctrl+T anyway — Excel will tell you if it's already done.

### Example prompts to try

**1. Get a plain-English summary of your data**
> *"Summarise this table. What are the top three trends and are there any figures that stand out as unusual?"*

Great for a quick read of a spreadsheet before a meeting, or when someone sends you data and asks for your view.

---

**2. Sort or filter without building a formula**
> *"Show me only the rows where the budget variance is more than 10%. Highlight them in the table."*

No need to set up a filter manually — just describe what you want to see.

---

**3. Ask a question about the numbers**
> *"Which department had the highest spend in Q2, and how does that compare to Q1? Show the result as a simple chart."*

Copilot will calculate the answer and generate the chart for you. Always check the numbers against the source — don't send a chart to a senior stakeholder without a quick sanity check first.

## A Word on Data Safety — Please Read

Microsoft 365 Copilot is part of our company systems — the same environment as your Outlook, Teams, and SharePoint. When you use it, everything stays inside FinBridge's own systems. Your prompts don't go to any external website, and Microsoft doesn't use your work content to train public AI models. You can use it with confidence.

The one thing to be aware of is the difference between Copilot and other AI tools you might come across online — things like general-purpose AI chat websites in your browser. Those are separate services, outside our company systems, and the rules are different.

**For those external tools, the habit is simple: don't paste work content into them.** Client names, financial figures, contract details — keep those inside our systems. This is the same principle from Day 1 of the DWP programme: if it belongs to FinBridge or a client, it stays in FinBridge's tools.

If you ever see an AI tool and aren't sure whether it's approved for work use, just drop IT a quick message before using it. That's not a sign you've done something wrong — it's exactly the right thing to do.

Bottom line: Copilot inside Microsoft 365 is where you should be working, and you're already there.

## Five Quick Tips Before You Start

1. **Be specific.** "Summarise this" works, but "Summarise the key risks in bullet points for a non-specialist reader" works better.
2. **Tell it the format you want.** Bullets, paragraphs, a table, a short email — say so and you'll get it.
3. **Always read the output before you use it.** Copilot is fast but not perfect. You are responsible for what you send.
4. **If the first result isn't right, ask again.** Try: *"That's too formal — can you make it more conversational?"* or *"Can you shorten that to three sentences?"*
5. **Start small.** Pick one task you do every week — a recurring email, a weekly summary, a data check — and try Copilot on that first. Build from there.
